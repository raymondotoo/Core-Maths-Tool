# Chapter 32: Mensuration

## 32.1 Prisms

A **prism** is a solid with uniform cross-section throughout its length.

**Volume of prism = Area of cross-section × length (height)**

**Surface area = 2 × Area of cross-section + Perimeter of cross-section × length**

### Example 32.1: Rectangular Prism (Cuboid)
#### Problem
A cuboid measures 8 cm × 5 cm × 4 cm. Find its volume and surface area.

![Cuboid](www/images/chapter_32/cuboid.svg)

#### Solution
Volume = 8 × 5 × 4 = **160 cm³**

Surface area = 2(8×5 + 8×4 + 5×4)
= 2(40 + 32 + 20)
= 2 × 92
= **184 cm²**

### Example 32.2: Triangular Prism
#### Problem
A triangular prism has a right-angled triangular cross-section with legs 6 cm and 8 cm. The prism length is 12 cm. Find the volume and surface area.

#### Solution
Area of triangle = ½ × 6 × 8 = 24 cm²
Hypotenuse = √(36 + 64) = 10 cm

Volume = 24 × 12 = **288 cm³**

Surface area = 2(24) + (6 + 8 + 10) × 12
= 48 + 288
= **336 cm²**

## 32.2 Cube

For a cube with side length a:

$$\text{Volume} = a^3$$
$$\text{Surface area} = 6a^2$$
$$\text{Diagonal of cube} = a\sqrt{3}$$

### Example 32.3: Cube Calculations
#### Problem
A cube has volume 343 cm³. Find its surface area and the length of its space diagonal.

#### Solution
a³ = 343
a = 7 cm

Surface area = 6 × 7² = 6 × 49 = **294 cm²**

Space diagonal = 7√3 = **12.1 cm** (3 s.f.)

## 32.3 Cylinder

For a cylinder with radius r and height h:

$$\text{Volume} = \pi r^2 h$$
$$\text{Curved surface area} = 2\pi r h$$
$$\text{Total surface area} = 2\pi r h + 2\pi r^2 = 2\pi r(h + r)$$

### Example 32.4: Cylinder Volume
#### Problem
A cylindrical tank has radius 3.5 m and height 4 m. Find its capacity in liters.

#### Solution
Volume = π × 3.5² × 4
= π × 12.25 × 4
= 49π
= 153.94 m³

Capacity = 153.94 × 1000 = **153,940 liters**

### Example 32.5: Cylinder Surface Area
#### Problem
Find the total surface area of a cylinder with radius 7 cm and height 10 cm.

![Cylinder](www/images/chapter_32/cylinder.svg)

#### Solution
Total surface area = 2πr(h + r)
= 2 × π × 7 × (10 + 7)
= 2 × π × 7 × 17
= 238π
= **747.7 cm²**

## 32.4 Cone

![Cone](www/images/chapter_32/cone.svg)

For a cone with radius r, height h, and slant height l:

$$l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$$
$$\text{Volume} = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$$
$$\text{Curved surface area} = \pi r l$$
$$\text{Total surface area} = \pi r l + \pi r^2 = \pi r(l + r)$$

### Example 32.6: Cone Volume
#### Problem
A cone has base radius 6 cm and height 8 cm. Find its volume.

#### Solution
Volume = ⅓ × π × 6² × 8
= ⅓ × π × 36 × 8
= 96π
= **301.6 cm³**

### Example 32.7: Cone Surface Area
#### Problem
A cone has radius 5 cm and height 12 cm. Find its total surface area.

#### Solution
Slant height l = √(5² + 12²) = √(25 + 144) = √169 = 13 cm

Total surface area = πr(l + r)
= π × 5 × (13 + 5)
= π × 5 × 18
= 90π
= **282.7 cm²**

## 32.5 Sphere

![Sphere](www/images/chapter_32/sphere.svg)

For a sphere with radius r:

$$\text{Volume} = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$$
$$\text{Surface area} = 4\pi r^2$$

### Example 32.8: Sphere Calculations
#### Problem
A sphere has diameter 14 cm. Find its volume and surface area.

#### Solution
Radius r = 7 cm

Volume = (4/3) × π × 7³
= (4/3) × π × 343
= (1372/3)π
= **1436.8 cm³**

Surface area = 4 × π × 7²
= 4 × π × 49
= 196π
= **615.8 cm²**

---

## 32.6 Hemisphere

For a hemisphere with radius r:

$$\text{Volume} = \frac{2}{3}\pi r^3$$
$$\text{Curved surface area} = 2\pi r^2$$
$$\text{Total surface area} = 2\pi r^2 + \pi r^2 = 3\pi r^2$$

### Example 32.9: Hemisphere
#### Problem
A hemispherical bowl has radius 10 cm. Find its total surface area.

#### Solution
Total surface area = 3πr²
= 3 × π × 10²
= 300π
= **942.5 cm²**

---

## 32.7 Pyramid

For a pyramid with base area A and height h:

$$\text{Volume} = \frac{1}{3} \times \text{Base area} \times \text{Height}$$

### Example 32.10: Rectangular Pyramid
#### Problem
A pyramid has a rectangular base 8 cm by 6 cm and height 9 cm. Find its volume.

#### Solution
Base area = 8 × 6 = 48 cm²
Volume = ⅓ × 48 × 9 = **144 cm³**

### Example 32.11: Square Pyramid Surface Area
#### Problem
A square pyramid has base side 10 cm and slant height 13 cm. Find its total surface area.

#### Solution
Base area = 10² = 100 cm²
Each triangular face = ½ × 10 × 13 = 65 cm²
4 triangular faces = 4 × 65 = 260 cm²

Total surface area = 100 + 260 = **360 cm²**

## 32.8 Frustum

A **frustum** is formed when a smaller similar cone or pyramid is removed from the top of a larger one.

### Example 32.12: Frustum of a Cone
#### Problem
A frustum of a cone has top radius 3 cm, base radius 6 cm, and height 8 cm. Find its volume.

#### Solution
Method: Use similar triangles to find the original cone height.

Scale factor = 6/3 = 2
The cut-off cone has height h, and full cone has height h + 8
So: (h + 8)/h = 2
h + 8 = 2h
h = 8 cm

Volume of full cone = ⅓ × π × 6² × 16 = 192π
Volume of small cone = ⅓ × π × 3² × 8 = 24π

Volume of frustum = 192π - 24π = 168π = **527.8 cm³**

**Or use formula:**
$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi h(R^2 + Rr + r^2)$$
= ⅓ × π × 8 × (36 + 18 + 9)
= ⅓ × π × 8 × 63
= 168π = **527.8 cm³**

## 32.9 Composite Solids

Composite solids combine two or more basic shapes.

### Example 32.13: Cylinder with Hemisphere
#### Problem
A solid consists of a cylinder of radius 4 cm and height 10 cm with a hemisphere of the same radius attached to one end. Find the total volume.

#### Solution
Volume of cylinder = π × 4² × 10 = 160π
Volume of hemisphere = (2/3) × π × 4³ = (128/3)π

Total volume = 160π + (128/3)π
= (480/3)π + (128/3)π
= (608/3)π
= **636.7 cm³**

## 32.10 Converting Units

### Length
1 km = 1000 m, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 cm = 10 mm

### Area
1 m² = 10,000 cm², 1 km² = 1,000,000 m²

### Volume
1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³, 1 liter = 1000 cm³

### Example 32.14: Unit Conversion
#### Problem
A tank holds 5 m³. How many liters does it hold?

#### Solution
5 m³ = 5 × 1,000,000 cm³ = 5,000,000 cm³
= 5,000,000 ÷ 1000 liters
= **5000 liters**
