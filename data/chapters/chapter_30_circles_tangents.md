# Chapter 30: Circle Theorems and Tangents

## 30.1 Circle Terms

- **Center**: Fixed point equidistant from all points on circle
- **Radius**: Distance from center to any point on circle
- **Diameter**: Line through center connecting two points on circle (d = 2r)
- **Chord**: Line segment joining any two points on circle
- **Secant**: Line that crosses circle at two points
- **Tangent**: Line that touches circle at exactly one point
- **Arc**: Part of the circumference
- **Sector**: Region bounded by two radii and an arc
- **Segment**: Region bounded by a chord and an arc

---

## 30.2 Angle at Center and Circumference

![Angle at Center](www/images/chapter_30/angle_at_center.svg)

**Theorem**: The angle at the center is twice the angle at the circumference when subtended by the same arc.

$$\text{Angle at center} = 2 \times \text{Angle at circumference}$$

### Example 30.1: Center and Circumference
#### Problem
The angle at the center of a circle is 80°. Find the angle at the circumference subtended by the same arc.

#### Solution
Angle at circumference = 80° ÷ 2 = **40°**

### Example 30.2: Finding Center Angle
#### Problem
An angle at the circumference is 55°. Find the angle at the center.

#### Solution
Angle at center = 2 × 55° = **110°**

---

## 30.3 Angle in a Semicircle

![Angle in Semicircle](www/images/chapter_30/angle_semicircle.svg)

**Theorem**: The angle in a semicircle is always 90°.

If AB is a diameter and C is any point on the circle, then angle ACB = 90°.

### Example 30.3: Semicircle Angle
#### Problem
AB is the diameter of a circle and C is a point on the circle. If angle BAC = 35°, find angle ABC.

#### Solution
Angle ACB = 90° (angle in semicircle)
Angle ABC = 180° - 90° - 35° = **55°**

---

## 30.4 Angles in the Same Segment

**Theorem**: Angles subtended by the same arc at the circumference are equal.

### Example 30.4: Same Segment
#### Problem
Points A, B, C, D lie on a circle. If angle CAD = 45°, find angle CBD.

#### Solution
Angles CAD and CBD are in the same segment (both subtended by arc CD).
Angle CBD = **45°**

---

## 30.5 Cyclic Quadrilateral

A **cyclic quadrilateral** has all four vertices on a circle.

**Theorem**: Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°.

### Example 30.5: Cyclic Quadrilateral
#### Problem
ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. Angle A = 85° and angle B = 100°. Find angles C and D.

![Cyclic Quadrilateral](www/images/chapter_30/cyclic_quadrilateral.svg)

#### Solution
A + C = 180° → C = 180° - 85° = **95°**
B + D = 180° → D = 180° - 100° = **80°**

### Example 30.6: Proving Cyclic
#### Problem
If opposite angles of quadrilateral PQRS are supplementary, what can you conclude?

#### Solution
The quadrilateral is **cyclic** (lies on a circle).

## 30.6 Tangent Properties

**Property 1**: A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.

**Property 2**: Tangents from an external point are equal in length.

### Example 30.7: Tangent and Radius
#### Problem
A tangent touches a circle at point P. The radius OP = 5 cm and the tangent meets a line through O at point T such that OT = 13 cm. Find PT.

![Tangent and Radius](www/images/chapter_30/tangent_radius.svg)

#### Solution
Angle OPT = 90° (tangent ⊥ radius)

Using Pythagoras:
PT² + OP² = OT²
PT² + 25 = 169
PT² = 144
PT = **12 cm**

### Example 30.8: Equal Tangents
#### Problem
From point T, tangents TA and TB are drawn to a circle. If TA = 8 cm, find TB.

#### Solution
Tangents from same external point are equal.
TB = **8 cm**

---

## 30.7 Alternate Segment Theorem

**Theorem**: The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.

### Example 30.9: Alternate Segment
#### Problem
A tangent at point A makes an angle of 50° with chord AB. Find the angle ACB where C is a point on the circle in the alternate segment.

#### Solution
By alternate segment theorem:
Angle ACB = **50°**

---

## 30.8 Intersecting Chords

**Theorem**: When two chords intersect inside a circle:
$$AP \times PB = CP \times PD$$

### Example 30.10: Intersecting Chords
#### Problem
Chords AB and CD intersect at P inside a circle. AP = 4 cm, PB = 6 cm, CP = 3 cm. Find PD.

#### Solution
AP × PB = CP × PD
4 × 6 = 3 × PD
24 = 3 × PD
PD = **8 cm**

---

## 30.9 Secant-Tangent Theorem

When a tangent and secant are drawn from external point:
$$\text{(Tangent)}^2 = \text{(Whole secant)} \times \text{(External part)}$$

### Example 30.11: Secant-Tangent
#### Problem
From point P, a tangent PT = 6 cm and a secant PAB where PA = 4 cm and AB passes through the circle. If PB = 9 cm, verify the theorem.

#### Solution
PT² = PA × PB
6² = 4 × 9
36 = 36 ✓

---

## 30.10 Two Secants from External Point

$$PA \times PB = PC \times PD$$

### Example 30.12: Two Secants
#### Problem
From external point P, secants PAB and PCD are drawn. PA = 3 cm, AB = 7 cm, PC = 4 cm. Find CD.

#### Solution
PB = PA + AB = 3 + 7 = 10 cm

PA × PB = PC × PD
3 × 10 = 4 × PD
30 = 4 × PD
PD = 7.5 cm

CD = PD - PC = 7.5 - 4 = **3.5 cm**

---

## 30.11 Finding the Center

Methods to find the center of a circle:
1. Draw any two chords
2. Construct perpendicular bisector of each chord
3. The bisectors intersect at the center

## Practice Exercises

1. The angle at the center is 100°. Find the angle at the circumference.

2. An angle at the circumference is 40°. Find:
   (a) The angle at the center
   (b) Another angle at circumference subtended by the same arc

3. AB is a diameter. C is on the circle. If angle BAC = 28°, find angle ABC.

4. ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral with angle A = 78° and angle D = 115°. Find angles B and C.

5. From point P outside a circle, tangents PA and PB are drawn. If angle APB = 60° and PA = 10 cm, find the length of AB.

6. A tangent at A makes an angle of 65° with chord AB. Find the angle ACB in the alternate segment.

7. Chords PQ and RS intersect at T inside a circle. PT = 5, TQ = 6, RT = 3. Find TS.

8. From external point X, a tangent XY = 8 cm and secant XAB with XA = 4 cm. Find AB.

9. Two secants from point P intersect circles at A, B and C, D. If PA = 4, AB = 5, PC = 3, find CD.

10. In a circle, the angle in a semicircle and one other angle of an inscribed triangle are known. Explain how to find all angles.
