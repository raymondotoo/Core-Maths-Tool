# Chapter 35: Average Speed, Changing Units and Scale

## 35.1 Speed, Distance and Time

$$\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}$$

$$\text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time}$$

$$\text{Time} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}}$$

### Units
- Speed: m/s, km/h, mph
- Distance: m, km, miles
- Time: seconds, hours

### Example 35.1: Finding Speed
#### Problem
A car travels 180 km in 3 hours. Find its average speed.

#### Solution
Speed = Distance ÷ Time
= 180 ÷ 3
= **60 km/h**

### Example 35.2: Finding Distance
#### Problem
A cyclist travels at 15 km/h for 2.5 hours. How far does she travel?

#### Solution
Distance = Speed × Time
= 15 × 2.5
= **37.5 km**

### Example 35.3: Finding Time
#### Problem
A train travels 450 km at an average speed of 90 km/h. How long does the journey take?

#### Solution
Time = Distance ÷ Speed
= 450 ÷ 90
= **5 hours**

---

## 35.2 Average Speed for Multiple Stages

$$\text{Average speed} = \frac{\text{Total distance}}{\text{Total time}}$$

### Example 35.4: Two-Part Journey
#### Problem
A car travels 100 km at 50 km/h, then another 80 km at 40 km/h. Find the average speed for the whole journey.

#### Solution
Time for first part = 100 ÷ 50 = 2 hours
Time for second part = 80 ÷ 40 = 2 hours
Total time = 4 hours
Total distance = 180 km

Average speed = 180 ÷ 4 = **45 km/h**

Note: The average speed is NOT (50 + 40) ÷ 2 = 45 km/h in general. It happened to be equal here.

### Example 35.5: There and Back
#### Problem
A cyclist travels 30 km uphill at 10 km/h and returns the same distance downhill at 30 km/h. Find the average speed for the round trip.

#### Solution
Time uphill = 30 ÷ 10 = 3 hours
Time downhill = 30 ÷ 30 = 1 hour
Total time = 4 hours
Total distance = 60 km

Average speed = 60 ÷ 4 = **15 km/h**

## 35.3 Converting Units

### Length
- 1 km = 1000 m
- 1 m = 100 cm
- 1 cm = 10 mm
- 1 mile ≈ 1.6 km

### Time
- 1 hour = 60 minutes
- 1 minute = 60 seconds
- 1 hour = 3600 seconds

### Converting km/h to m/s
$$\text{m/s} = \text{km/h} \times \frac{1000}{3600} = \text{km/h} \times \frac{5}{18}$$

### Converting m/s to km/h
$$\text{km/h} = \text{m/s} \times \frac{3600}{1000} = \text{m/s} \times \frac{18}{5}$$

### Example 35.6: Converting Speed Units
#### Problem
Convert: (a) 72 km/h to m/s (b) 15 m/s to km/h

#### Solution
(a) 72 km/h = 72 × (5/18) = **20 m/s**

(b) 15 m/s = 15 × (18/5) = **54 km/h**

---

## 35.4 Converting Area and Volume Units

### Area
- 1 m² = 10,000 cm² = 10⁴ cm²
- 1 km² = 1,000,000 m² = 10⁶ m²
- 1 hectare = 10,000 m²

### Volume
- 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³ = 10⁶ cm³
- 1 liter = 1000 cm³ = 1 dm³
- 1 m³ = 1000 liters

### Example 35.7: Area Conversions
#### Problem
Convert 5.4 m² to cm².

#### Solution
5.4 m² = 5.4 × 10,000 cm² = **54,000 cm²**

### Example 35.8: Volume Conversions
#### Problem
A tank has capacity 2.5 m³. How many liters is this?

#### Solution
2.5 m³ = 2.5 × 1000 liters = **2500 liters**

## 35.5 Scale Diagrams

A **scale** shows the ratio of lengths on a diagram to actual lengths.

### Common Scale Formats
- 1:50,000 (1 cm represents 50,000 cm = 500 m)
- 1 cm to 100 m
- 2 cm represents 5 km

### Example 35.9: Reading a Map
#### Problem
A map has scale 1:25,000. Two towns are 6 cm apart on the map. Find the actual distance in km.

#### Solution
Actual distance = 6 × 25,000 cm
= 150,000 cm
= 1,500 m
= **1.5 km**

### Example 35.10: Drawing to Scale
#### Problem
A room is 8 m by 5 m. Using scale 1 cm to 2 m, find the dimensions of the scale drawing.

#### Solution
Length = 8 ÷ 2 = **4 cm**
Width = 5 ÷ 2 = **2.5 cm**

---

## 35.6 Area and Scale

If the linear scale factor is k, the area scale factor is k².

### Example 35.11: Area on Maps
#### Problem
A map has scale 1:50,000. A lake appears as 4 cm² on the map. Find its actual area in km².

#### Solution
Linear scale factor = 50,000
Area scale factor = 50,000² = 2.5 × 10⁹

Actual area = 4 × 2.5 × 10⁹ cm²
= 10 × 10⁹ cm²
= 10 × 10⁹ ÷ 10¹⁰ km²
= **1 km²**

---

## 35.7 Distance-Time Graphs

- **Horizontal line**: Stationary (not moving)
- **Slope** = speed
- **Steeper slope** = faster speed

### Example 35.12: Interpreting Distance-Time Graph
#### Problem
A distance-time graph shows:
- 0 to 1 hour: Distance increases from 0 to 40 km
- 1 to 2 hours: Distance stays at 40 km
- 2 to 3 hours: Distance increases from 40 to 70 km

Describe the journey.

#### Solution
- First hour: Traveling at 40 km/h
- Second hour: Stopped (rest)
- Third hour: Traveling at 30 km/h

Average speed for whole journey = 70 ÷ 3 = **23.3 km/h**

---

## 35.8 Speed-Time Graphs

- **Area under graph** = distance traveled
- **Positive slope** = acceleration
- **Negative slope** = deceleration
- **Horizontal line** = constant speed

### Example 35.13: Finding Distance from Speed-Time Graph
#### Problem
A car accelerates uniformly from rest to 20 m/s in 10 seconds, then travels at constant speed for 20 seconds. Find the total distance.

#### Solution
Area = Triangle + Rectangle
= ½ × 10 × 20 + 20 × 20
= 100 + 400
= **500 m**

---

## 35.9 Relative Speed

### Same Direction
Relative speed = |Speed₁ − Speed₂|

### Opposite Direction
Relative speed = Speed₁ + Speed₂

### Example 35.14: Catching Up
#### Problem
Car A travels at 80 km/h and Car B at 60 km/h in the same direction. If A is 40 km behind B, how long until A catches up?

#### Solution
Relative speed = 80 − 60 = 20 km/h
Time = Distance ÷ Relative speed
= 40 ÷ 20
= **2 hours**
