# Chapter 37: Global Mathematics (Earth Geometry)

## 37.1 The Earth as a Sphere

The Earth is approximately a sphere with:
- Radius ≈ 6,400 km (or 3,960 miles)
- Circumference ≈ 40,000 km (or 24,900 miles)

### Key Terms
- **Great circle**: Any circle on a sphere whose center is at the center of the sphere (radius = Earth's radius)
- **Small circle**: Any other circle on the sphere
- **Equator**: The great circle equidistant from both poles
- **Meridian (Longitude)**: Great circles passing through both poles
- **Parallel of Latitude**: Small circles parallel to the equator

---

## 37.2 Latitude and Longitude

### Latitude
- Angular distance north or south of the equator
- Ranges from 0° (equator) to 90°N or 90°S (poles)

### Longitude
- Angular distance east or west of the Prime Meridian (0°)
- Ranges from 0° to 180°E or 180°W

### Example 37.1: Reading Coordinates
#### Problem
A city is located at 52°N, 21°E. Describe its position.

#### Solution
The city is **52° north of the equator** and **21° east of the Prime Meridian**.

---

## 37.3 Distance Along a Meridian (Great Circle)

Distance along a meridian:
$$\text{Distance} = \frac{\theta}{360°} \times 2\pi R$$

Where θ is the angular difference in latitude and R is Earth's radius.

### Example 37.2: North-South Distance
#### Problem
Find the distance from a point at 15°N to another point at 45°N on the same meridian. (Earth's radius = 6,400 km)

#### Solution
Angular difference = 45° − 15° = 30°

Distance = (30/360) × 2 × π × 6400
= (1/12) × 2 × π × 6400
= (1/12) × 40,212
= **3,351 km**

---

## 37.4 Distance Along a Parallel of Latitude

The radius of a parallel at latitude φ:
$$r = R \cos\phi$$

Distance along a parallel:
$$\text{Distance} = \frac{\theta}{360°} \times 2\pi r = \frac{\theta}{360°} \times 2\pi R \cos\phi$$

Where θ is the angular difference in longitude.

### Example 37.3: East-West Distance
#### Problem
Find the distance between two points at 60°N latitude, one at 30°W and the other at 60°E. (R = 6,400 km)

#### Solution
Angular difference = 30° + 60° = 90°
r = 6400 × cos 60° = 6400 × 0.5 = 3200 km

Distance = (90/360) × 2 × π × 3200
= (1/4) × 2 × π × 3200
= (1/4) × 20,106
= **5,027 km**

## 37.5 Nautical Miles and Knots

### Nautical Mile
1 nautical mile = 1 minute of arc along a great circle
1 nautical mile ≈ 1.852 km

### Knot
1 knot = 1 nautical mile per hour

### Example 37.4: Using Nautical Miles
#### Problem
A ship sails from 10°N, 30°W to 25°N, 30°W. Find the distance in nautical miles.

#### Solution
Angular difference = 25° − 10° = 15°
= 15 × 60 minutes = 900 minutes

Distance = **900 nautical miles**

---

## 37.6 Shortest Distance on Earth

The shortest distance between two points on Earth is along the **great circle** passing through both points.

For points on the same meridian or on the equator, this is straightforward. For other points, spherical trigonometry is needed.

### Example 37.5: Shortest Route
#### Problem
Describe the shortest route from London (51°N, 0°) to Tokyo (35°N, 140°E).

#### Solution
The shortest route is along the great circle, which often goes **over the Arctic** rather than along the parallel of latitude.

## 37.7 Time Zones

- Earth rotates 360° in 24 hours = 15° per hour
- Time zones are approximately 15° wide
- Each 15° of longitude = 1 hour time difference

### Time Calculation
- Moving **east**: Add time
- Moving **west**: Subtract time

### Example 37.6: Time Zone Calculations
#### Problem
When it is 3:00 PM in London (0° longitude), what time is it in New York (75°W)?

#### Solution
Longitude difference = 75°
Time difference = 75 ÷ 15 = 5 hours

New York is west, so subtract:
3:00 PM − 5 hours = **10:00 AM**

### Example 37.7: Flight Time Problem
#### Problem
A plane leaves Lagos (6°N, 3°E) at 8:00 AM local time and arrives in London (51°N, 0°) at 2:00 PM local London time. Find the flight duration.

#### Solution
Time difference = 3° ÷ 15° = 0.2 hours ≈ 12 minutes
Lagos is east of London, so Lagos is 12 minutes ahead.

When it's 8:00 AM in Lagos, it's 7:48 AM in London.
Flight duration = 2:00 PM − 7:48 AM = **6 hours 12 minutes**

---

## 37.8 Speed of Rotation

At the equator, Earth's surface moves fastest. At latitude φ:
$$\text{Speed} = \frac{2\pi R \cos\phi}{24} \text{ km/h}$$

### Example 37.8: Rotational Speed
#### Problem
Find the speed of rotation at the equator. (R = 6,400 km)

#### Solution
Speed = (2 × π × 6400 × cos 0°) ÷ 24
= (2 × π × 6400 × 1) ÷ 24
= 40,212 ÷ 24
= **1,676 km/h**

---

## 37.9 The International Date Line

The International Date Line is approximately at 180° longitude.
- Crossing from west to east: Subtract one day
- Crossing from east to west: Add one day

### Example 37.9: Crossing the Date Line
#### Problem
A ship crosses the International Date Line eastward on Tuesday at noon. What day and time is it after crossing?

#### Solution
After crossing eastward: **Monday at noon** (go back one day).
