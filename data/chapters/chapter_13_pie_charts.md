# Chapter 13: Pie Charts

## 13.1 Introduction to Pie Charts

A **pie chart** is a circular graph divided into sectors, where each sector represents a proportion of the whole.

### Key Concepts:
- The whole circle = 360°
- Each sector's angle is proportional to its value
- Sector angle = (Value / Total) × 360°

---

## 13.2 Drawing Pie Charts

### Steps:
1. Find the total of all values
2. Calculate the angle for each sector: Angle = (Value ÷ Total) × 360°
3. Draw a circle and mark the center
4. Use a protractor to draw each sector
5. Label each sector

### Example 13.1: Drawing a Pie Chart
#### Problem
A student spends their day as follows:
- School: 8 hours
- Homework: 3 hours
- Sleep: 7 hours
- Other: 6 hours

Draw a pie chart to represent this data.

#### Solution
Total = 8 + 3 + 7 + 6 = 24 hours

Calculate angles:
- School: (8/24) × 360° = **120°**
- Homework: (3/24) × 360° = **45°**
- Sleep: (7/24) × 360° = **105°**
- Other: (6/24) × 360° = **90°**

Check: 120° + 45° + 105° + 90° = 360° ✓

Draw circle and sectors with these angles.

## 13.3 Reading Pie Charts

### Example 13.2: Interpreting a Pie Chart
#### Problem
In a pie chart showing favorite fruits of 60 students, the sector for mangoes has an angle of 90°. How many students prefer mangoes?

#### Solution
Sector angle / 360° = Number preferring mangoes / Total students

$$\frac{90}{360} = \frac{x}{60}$$

$$x = \frac{90 \times 60}{360} = \frac{5400}{360} = \mathbf{15 \text{ students}}$$

### Example 13.3: Finding Values from Angles
#### Problem
A company's annual expenditure is represented in a pie chart. If the sector for salaries has an angle of 144° and the total expenditure is GH₵500,000, how much was spent on salaries?

#### Solution
$$\text{Salaries} = \frac{144}{360} \times 500,000$$
$$= 0.4 \times 500,000$$
$$= \mathbf{GH₵200,000}$$

## 13.4 Comparing Data Using Pie Charts

### Example 13.4: Comparison Problem
#### Problem
A pie chart shows grades in a class of 120 students:
- Grade A: 60°
- Grade B: 100°
- Grade C: 120°
- Grade D: 80°

(a) How many students got each grade?
(b) What fraction got Grade A or B?

#### Solution
(a) Calculations:
- Grade A: (60/360) × 120 = **20 students**
- Grade B: (100/360) × 120 = **33⅓ ≈ 33 students**
- Grade C: (120/360) × 120 = **40 students**
- Grade D: (80/360) × 120 = **26⅔ ≈ 27 students**

(b) Grades A or B angle = 60° + 100° = 160°
Fraction = 160/360 = **4/9**

## 13.5 Percentage and Pie Charts

### Converting Between Percentages and Angles:
- Angle to Percentage: (Angle/360) × 100%
- Percentage to Angle: (Percentage/100) × 360°

### Example 13.5: Percentages in Pie Charts
#### Problem
In a survey of 200 people about transport preference:
- Bus: 25%
- Car: 40%
- Bicycle: 15%
- Walking: 20%

Find the angle for each sector and the number of people.

#### Solution
Angles:
- Bus: (25/100) × 360° = **90°**
- Car: (40/100) × 360° = **144°**
- Bicycle: (15/100) × 360° = **54°**
- Walking: (20/100) × 360° = **72°**

Number of people:
- Bus: (25/100) × 200 = **50 people**
- Car: (40/100) × 200 = **80 people**
- Bicycle: (15/100) × 200 = **30 people**
- Walking: (20/100) × 200 = **40 people**

## 13.6 Finding Missing Values

### Example 13.6: Finding Unknown Angle
#### Problem
In a pie chart showing sales, three sectors have angles 85°, 110°, and 95°. Find the fourth sector's angle.

#### Solution
Sum of all angles = 360°
Fourth angle = 360° - (85° + 110° + 95°)
            = 360° - 290°
            = **70°**

### Example 13.7: Finding Unknown Value
#### Problem
A school budget pie chart shows:
- Salaries: GH₵180,000 (represented by 216°)
- Utilities: x (represented by 60°)
- Supplies: GH₵50,000 (represented by 60°)
- Other: y (represented by 24°)

Find the values of x and y.

#### Solution
First, find the total budget using salaries:
$$\frac{216}{360} \times \text{Total} = 180,000$$
$$\text{Total} = \frac{180,000 \times 360}{216} = \mathbf{GH₵300,000}$$

Utilities (x):
$$x = \frac{60}{360} \times 300,000 = \mathbf{GH₵50,000}$$

Other (y):
$$y = \frac{24}{360} \times 300,000 = \mathbf{GH₵20,000}$$

## 13.7 Constructing Pie Charts from Given Data

### Example 13.8: Complete Construction
#### Problem
A farmer has livestock as follows:
- Cattle: 45
- Goats: 30
- Sheep: 35
- Poultry: 70

Construct a pie chart for this data.

#### Solution
Total = 45 + 30 + 35 + 70 = 180

Calculate angles:
- Cattle: (45/180) × 360° = **90°**
- Goats: (30/180) × 360° = **60°**
- Sheep: (35/180) × 360° = **70°**
- Poultry: (70/180) × 360° = **140°**

Verification: 90° + 60° + 70° + 140° = 360° ✓

| Animal | Number | Fraction | Angle |
|--------|--------|----------|-------|
| Cattle | 45 | 45/180 = 1/4 | 90° |
| Goats | 30 | 30/180 = 1/6 | 60° |
| Sheep | 35 | 35/180 = 7/36 | 70° |
| Poultry | 70 | 70/180 = 7/18 | 140° |

## 13.8 Word Problems

### Example 13.9: Budget Problem
#### Problem
A family's monthly budget is shown in a pie chart. Food is represented by 108°, rent by 90°, transport by 54°, and savings by the remaining sector. If the total income is GH₵3,600:

(a) Find the angle for savings
(b) Calculate the amount spent on each category

#### Solution
(a) Savings angle = 360° - (108° + 90° + 54°)
                  = 360° - 252°
                  = **108°**

(b) Food: (108/360) × 3600 = **GH₵1,080**
    Rent: (90/360) × 3600 = **GH₵900**
    Transport: (54/360) × 3600 = **GH₵540**
    Savings: (108/360) × 3600 = **GH₵1,080**
