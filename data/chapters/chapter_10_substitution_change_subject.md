# Chapter 10: Substitution and Change of Subject

## 10.1 Substitution into Formulae

**Substitution** means replacing variables with given values and evaluating the expression.

### Example 10.1: Simple Substitution
#### Problem
If a = 3 and b = 4, find the value of 2a + 3b

#### Solution
2a + 3b = 2(3) + 3(4)
       = 6 + 12
       = **18**

### Example 10.2: Substitution with Powers
#### Problem
If x = 2 and y = -3, find the value of x² + 2xy + y²

#### Solution
x² + 2xy + y² = (2)² + 2(2)(-3) + (-3)²
              = 4 + (-12) + 9
              = 4 - 12 + 9
              = **1**

### Example 10.3: Formula Evaluation
#### Problem
The formula for the area of a trapezium is $$A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$$

Find A when a = 5 cm, b = 9 cm, and h = 6 cm.

#### Solution
$$A = \frac{1}{2}(5+9)(6)$$
$$A = \frac{1}{2}(14)(6)$$
$$A = \frac{1}{2}(84)$$
$$A = \mathbf{42 \text{ cm}^2}$$

## 10.2 Change of Subject (Basic)

**Changing the subject** means rearranging a formula to express a different variable in terms of the others.

### Steps:
1. Identify the new subject
2. Isolate it on one side of the equation
3. Use inverse operations

### Example 10.4: Single Operation
#### Problem
Make x the subject of y = x + 5

#### Solution
y = x + 5
Subtract 5 from both sides:
y - 5 = x

**x = y - 5**

### Example 10.5: Division
#### Problem
Make r the subject of C = 2πr

#### Solution
C = 2πr
Divide both sides by 2π:
$$\frac{C}{2\pi} = r$$

$$\mathbf{r = \frac{C}{2\pi}}$$

## 10.3 Change of Subject with Multiple Operations

### Example 10.6: Two Operations
#### Problem
Make x the subject of y = 3x - 7

#### Solution
y = 3x - 7

Step 1: Add 7 to both sides
y + 7 = 3x

Step 2: Divide by 3
$$\frac{y+7}{3} = x$$

$$\mathbf{x = \frac{y+7}{3}}$$

### Example 10.7: Subject in Denominator
#### Problem
Make t the subject of $$v = \frac{d}{t}$$

#### Solution
$$v = \frac{d}{t}$$

Multiply both sides by t:
vt = d

Divide by v:
$$\mathbf{t = \frac{d}{v}}$$

## 10.4 Subject Appears in Multiple Terms

When the subject appears more than once, collect all terms containing it on one side and factor.

### Example 10.8: Subject Appears Twice
#### Problem
Make x the subject of ax + b = cx + d

#### Solution
ax + b = cx + d

Step 1: Collect x terms on one side
ax - cx = d - b

Step 2: Factor out x
x(a - c) = d - b

Step 3: Divide by (a - c)
$$\mathbf{x = \frac{d-b}{a-c}}$$

### Example 10.9: More Complex
#### Problem
Make y the subject of $$\frac{y+a}{y-b} = c$$

#### Solution
$$\frac{y+a}{y-b} = c$$

Step 1: Multiply by (y - b)
y + a = c(y - b)

Step 2: Expand
y + a = cy - bc

Step 3: Collect y terms
y - cy = -bc - a

Step 4: Factor
y(1 - c) = -bc - a

Step 5: Divide
$$\mathbf{y = \frac{-bc-a}{1-c} = \frac{bc+a}{c-1}}$$

## 10.5 Change of Subject with Powers and Roots

### Example 10.10: Square Root Required
#### Problem
Make r the subject of A = πr²

#### Solution
A = πr²

Divide by π:
$$\frac{A}{\pi} = r^2$$

Take square root:
$$\mathbf{r = \sqrt{\frac{A}{\pi}}}$$

(Assuming r > 0)

### Example 10.11: Square Required
#### Problem
Make x the subject of y = √(x + 3)

#### Solution
y = √(x + 3)

Square both sides:
y² = x + 3

Subtract 3:
**x = y² - 3**

### Example 10.12: Cube Root
#### Problem
Make r the subject of $$V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$$

#### Solution
$$V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$$

Multiply by $$\frac{3}{4\pi}$$:
$$\frac{3V}{4\pi} = r^3$$

Take cube root:
$$\mathbf{r = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3V}{4\pi}}}$$

## 10.6 Formulae with Fractions

### Example 10.13: Clearing Fractions
#### Problem
Make u the subject of $$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{u} + \frac{1}{v}$$

#### Solution
$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{u} + \frac{1}{v}$$

Rearrange:
$$\frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{v}$$

$$\frac{1}{u} = \frac{v-f}{fv}$$

Take reciprocal:
$$\mathbf{u = \frac{fv}{v-f}}$$

### Example 10.14: Complex Fraction
#### Problem
Make b the subject of $$A = \frac{a+b}{a-b}$$

#### Solution
$$A = \frac{a+b}{a-b}$$

Multiply both sides by (a - b):
A(a - b) = a + b
Aa - Ab = a + b

Collect b terms:
Aa - a = Ab + b
Aa - a = b(A + 1)

Divide:
$$\mathbf{b = \frac{a(A-1)}{A+1}}$$

## 10.7 Word Problems

### Example 10.15: Speed Formula
#### Problem
The formula s = d/t relates speed (s), distance (d), and time (t).
(a) Make t the subject
(b) Find t when d = 120 km and s = 40 km/h

#### Solution
(a) s = d/t
    st = d
    **t = d/s**

(b) t = 120/40
    **t = 3 hours**

### Example 10.16: Temperature Conversion
#### Problem
The formula relating Celsius (C) and Fahrenheit (F) is $$F = \frac{9C}{5} + 32$$

(a) Make C the subject
(b) Convert 77°F to Celsius

#### Solution
(a) $$F = \frac{9C}{5} + 32$$
    $$F - 32 = \frac{9C}{5}$$
    $$5(F - 32) = 9C$$
    $$\mathbf{C = \frac{5(F-32)}{9}}$$

(b) $$C = \frac{5(77-32)}{9} = \frac{5(45)}{9} = \frac{225}{9} = \mathbf{25°C}$$

## 10.8 Practical Formulae

### Example 10.17: Simple Interest
#### Problem
Simple Interest is given by $$I = \frac{PRT}{100}$$

Make R the subject and find R if I = 150, P = 2500, and T = 2.

#### Solution
$$I = \frac{PRT}{100}$$

100I = PRT

$$\mathbf{R = \frac{100I}{PT}}$$

$$R = \frac{100 \times 150}{2500 \times 2} = \frac{15000}{5000} = \mathbf{3\%}$$

### Example 10.18: Kinematic Equation
#### Problem
Given the equation v² = u² + 2as, make s the subject.

#### Solution
v² = u² + 2as
v² - u² = 2as
$$\mathbf{s = \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2a}}$$
